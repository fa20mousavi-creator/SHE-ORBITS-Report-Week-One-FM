# Literature Review — GNSS Antenna Phase Center

## Objective

The purpose of this review is to understand GNSS antenna phase-center
behavior and identify the physical quantities that are relevant to
antenna stability analysis.

## 1. GNSS Antenna Phase Center

The GNSS antenna does not have a fixed electrical reference point.
The phase center varies with the direction of the incoming signal.

The phase-center behavior is commonly represented using:

- Phase Center Offset (PCO)
- Phase Center Variation (PCV)

PCV depends on the azimuth and elevation of the incoming GNSS signal.

## 2. Reference Magnitude of PCV

The U.S. National Geodetic Survey reports that the average phase-center
offset is typically on the order of a few centimeters, while phase-center
variations can reach approximately 4–5 mm for GNSS antennas.

## 3. Effect on Positioning

Published research has shown that incorrect or ignored antenna
phase-center corrections can produce significant positioning errors,
particularly in the height component.

One study reported maximum variations of approximately:

- 8 cm in height
- 4 cm in northing

under the investigated conditions.

## 4. Relevance to This Project

The thermal/structural model developed in this project is intended to
provide a physical stability check for the GNSS antenna.

The key question is whether thermal loading can produce sufficient
mechanical deformation to cause a meaningful phase-center displacement.
